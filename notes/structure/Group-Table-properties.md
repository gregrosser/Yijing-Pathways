### 1. Vertical symmetry — row-twins across axes

Yang/yin flipping lines {1,2,3} (inner trigram) or {4,5,6} (outer trigram)
of a hexagram in row *n* of one axis table always produces its
**row-twin**: the hexagram in row *n* of the *other* axis table. Confirmed
with zero exceptions across all 64 hexagrams.

#### The three-flip pattern (2026-08-09)

The artifact's own dual-pair attribution (its `data-link` bracket data) is
fully consistent on both axes: all six quartet rows pair up exactly as
row *n* ↔ row (11−*n*) — 1↔10, 2↔9, 3↔8 — with no exceptions. Row 3↔8
isn't an exception to that duality; it's simply not reached by the {1,6}
flip specifically. There are three possible symmetric line-pair flips —
{1,6} (outer edges), {2,5} (middle pair), {3,4} (inner boundary pair) —
and each attributed quartet dual pair is realized by exactly two of the
three, with each flip type in turn serving exactly two of the three
quartet pairs (right table above).

Extending the same check to the complementary pairs (rows 4–7, the
two-member rows) — dual pairs row 4↔7 and row 5↔6 — the pattern is
sharper still: only {1,6} connects them. {2,5} and {3,4} connect neither,
confirmed on both axes with no exceptions.

Confirmed exhaustively (all members, both axis tables). Across all five
dual pairs, {1,6} is the only flip that never fails — it connects every
dual pair except 3↔8, which is instead the one pair where both {2,5} and
{3,4} succeed.

#### The pair-block as index (2026-08-09)

Four rows (4–7) have exactly three ways to split into two twosomes.
Checking each possible pairing of rows 4–7 against the same three flips —
not just the row-(11−*n*) dual 4↔7/5↔6 above, but also 4↔6/5↔7 and
4↔5/6↔7 (left table above) — shows each of the three pairings is realized
by *exactly one* flip, cleanly, with no overlap (unlike the quartets,
where each dual needs two of the three flips). Confirmed on both axes, no
exceptions.

This isn't a separate fact sitting next to the quartet pattern — it
indexes it. The flip that *fails* for a given quartet dual is exactly the
flip that *realizes* one specific pairing of rows 4–7:

- Quartet dual 1↔10 fails {3,4} — the flip pairing 4↔5, 6↔7
- Quartet dual 2↔9 fails {2,5} — the flip pairing 4↔6, 5↔7
- Quartet dual 3↔8 fails {1,6} — the flip pairing 4↔7, 5↔6

So the pair-block's internal three-matching structure sits at the center
of the whole table's flip symmetry, not just at the center of its printed
layout: which flip a quartet dual excludes is read off directly from
which pairing of rows 4–7 that same flip produces.

### 3. Monotonic ordering (2026-08-09)

Reading top to bottom on either axis, both row-blocks turn out to be
sorted by their own arithmetic: within the six quartet rows (1, 2, 3, 8,
9, 10), the four FX numbers in each row sum to a total that only ever
increases; within the four pair rows (4, 5, 6, 7), the same holds for
their two FX numbers — two independent increasing sequences, not one
continuous one down the whole table. Confirmed on both axes, with a
single exception in the quartet block: rows 3 and 8 tie rather than
increasing.

| row | FX(31,32) group | sum | FX(33,34) group | sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FX{9,10,17,18} | 54 | FX{7,8,31,32} | 78 |
| 2 | FX{5,6,33,34} | 78 | FX{11,12,47,48} | 118 |
| 3 | FX{13,14,49,50} | 126 | FX{3,4,63,64} | 134 |
| 8 | FX{21,22,41,42} | 126 | FX{27,28,39,40} | 134 |
| 9 | FX{29,30,57,58} | 174 | FX{19,20,55,56} | 150 |
| 10 | FX{45,46,53,54} | 198 | FX{35,36,59,60} | 190 |

The tied pair is the same row 3 ↔ row 8 dual singled out in Observation
2's three-flip pattern — a second, independent property (arithmetic
rather than line-flip) landing on the exact same pair.

#### Why 3/8 ties

The tie isn't a coincidence — it's forced by a bracketing identity across
rows 3–8:

```
sum(row 3) = sum(row 4) + sum(row 7) = sum(row 5) + sum(row 6) = sum(row 8)
```

Confirmed exactly on both axes: LEFT — 126 = (3+123) = (51+75) = 126.
RIGHT — 134 = (31+103) = (47+87) = 134. Row 3 and row 8 both independently
equal the sum of the two bracketed pair-rows between them, which are
themselves equal to each other — so row 3 = row 8 follows necessarily.
1/10 and 2/9 don't tie because nothing analogous brackets them the same
way.

#### The pair rows, for comparison

Same check, no tie this time:

| row | FX(31,32) pair | sum | FX(33,34) pair | sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | FX{1,2} | 3 | FX{15,16} | 31 |
| 5 | FX{25,26} | 51 | FX{23,24} | 47 |
| 6 | FX{37,38} | 75 | FX{43,44} | 87 |
| 7 | FX{61,62} | 123 | FX{51,52} | 103 |

Row 4's sum (3, or 31) is far smaller than row 3's (126, or 134), so
quartet-block and pair-block sums sit on different scales — the
monotonic ordering holds within each block, not across the two combined.

#### A shared constant across all duals

The 3/8 bracketing identity above turns out to be one instance of a
larger invariant. All three quartet-dual sums — row 1+10, row 2+9, row
3+8 — are equal to each other *and* to the combined total of the entire
pair block (rows 4–7):

```
(row 1 + row 10) = (row 2 + row 9) = (row 3 + row 8) = (rows 4+5+6+7)
```

Confirmed exactly on both axes: LEFT — 54+198 = 78+174 = 126+126 =
3+51+75+123 = 252. RIGHT — 78+190 = 118+150 = 134+134 = 31+47+87+103 =
268. So the row 3/8 tie isn't a special case of this constant — it's the
one dual pair where the constant splits into two *equal* halves rather
than two different ones.

The 252/268 split is itself axis-specific; combining both axis columns
per row (LEFT+RIGHT) produces a single constant, 520, that holds
axis-independently:

| combined row (LEFT+RIGHT) | sum |
|---|---|
| row 1 | 132 |
| row 2 | 196 |
| row 3 | 260 |
| row 4 | 34 |
| row 5 | 98 |
| row 6 | 162 |
| row 7 | 226 |
| row 8 | 260 |
| row 9 | 324 |
| row 10 | 388 |

row1+row10 = row2+row9 = row3+row8 = rows4+5+6+7 = 520, confirmed exactly
(252+268 = 520). This isn't a coincidence of the FX-labeling scheme:
1+2+...+64 = 2080, and each of these four groups contains exactly 16
hexagrams across both axes combined — 2080÷4 = 520. So the identity
is really saying each of these four 16-hexagram partitions is perfectly
balanced around the global mean (32.5 per hexagram).

### 4. Perpendicular-pair reflection

Rows 1 and 3 are "Qian/Kun + mixed"; their dual partners, rows 8 and 10,
are "Li/Kan + mixed". This reflects the Xiantian trigram circle's two
perpendicular pairs (Qian↔Kun, Li↔Kan stand perpendicular to each other
on the circle). **Verified** — exhaustively true for every member of rows
1, 3, 8, 10 on both axis tables (not just the single spot-checked
instance from the original session): exactly one of {inner, outer}
trigram is in {Qian,Kun} (rows 1, 3) or {Li,Kan} (rows 8, 10); the other
is always one of the four mixed trigrams.

### 5. Row 2 / row 9 asymmetry

Row 2 is "Qian/Kun + Li/Kan" (both perpendicular-pair trigrams together);
its dual partner, row 9, is "mixed + mixed" — the one place where duality
does not preserve the same trigram-composition type. **Verified on
FX(31,32), breaks on FX(33,34).** On the FX(31,32) axis, the exact
correspondence is the 8-cycle rotation
Qian→Xun→Kan→Gen→Kun→Zhen→Li→Dui→Qian applied to both trigrams of every
row-2 member — this reproduces row 9's four members exactly. On the
FX(33,34) axis this breaks down: its row 9 (FX{19,20,55,56}) is the four
*doubled*-mixed-trigram hexagrams (Zhen/Zhen, Xun/Xun, Dui/Dui, Gen/Gen —
each hexagram's inner and outer trigram are identical), which no
per-trigram substitution mapping can produce from row 2's four
distinct-pair members. The rotation correspondence claimed in the
original observation is real but axis-specific, not a general property
of row 2/row 9 across both axes. Not yet resolved: what *does* connect
FX(33,34)'s row 2 to its row 9.

### 6. Axis definition via line 1 / line 6 (2026-08-09)

Checked directly against every hexagram's own line data (not derived
from FX numbers or sums): all 32 hexagrams on the FX(31,32) axis have
line 1 = line 6 (bottom and top lines match); all 32 hexagrams on the
FX(33,34) axis have line 1 ≠ line 6 (bottom and top lines differ). No
exceptions either way. This looks like the underlying definition of the
two axes themselves — the 64-hexagram set splits in half by whether its
outermost line pair agrees, and that split is exactly the
FX(31,32)/FX(33,34) partition used throughout this table.

## Background

The Xiantian trigram circle referenced above:
[Qian, Xun, Kan, Gen, Kun, Zhen, Li, Dui] at
[90, 45, 0, 315, 270, 225, 180, 135] degrees.

- Qian/Kun and Li/Kan (the four "self-reversing" trigrams — unchanged by
  line-order reversal) sit on one cross (0°/90°/180°/270°).
- Xun/Dui/Zhen/Gen (the "mixed", non-palindromic trigrams) sit on the other
  cross, rotated 45° from the first.

The FX Group Table's row structure is a direct reflection of this cross
geometry, not a coincidence.
